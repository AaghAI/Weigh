Help

Instructions

Each session

Pick your Site, confirm the Date, and set the anticipated weight range — tap a box and say the number, or just type it. Your settings for that site (range, comments, and the Individual I.D. option) are remembered automatically next time you pick it.

Voice check

A short 3-weight check confirms your phone is hearing you clearly before you start a session. If you're confident it's working fine, tap "Skip Voice Check and Proceed" instead.

Recording weights

Say the weight, then "over" — like a radio call.

If "Include Individual I.D." is switched on: say the I.D., then "over", then the weight, then "over" — two steps for each pig.

Prefer it to log automatically after a short pause instead of saying "over"? Switch to "Auto-log on pause" (this isn't available when Individual I.D. is on, since two steps need a clear signal between them).

Fixing mistakes

Every entry on the tally board can be edited or deleted — just tap a figure to correct it. Rows outlined in orange were flagged as a likely mishearing or outside your usual range; check those before exporting.

Working without a signal

Typed weights, editing, saving batches, and exporting all work with no signal at all. Voice recognition itself needs at least a weak connection to work reliably \u2014 if you're somewhere with none, switch to typing weights in manually; nothing is lost, and it all exports the same way later.

Finishing up

"Export to Excel" saves a spreadsheet with your Site, Date, range, and comments included at the top. It saves to wherever your phone's browser saves downloads — usually a Downloads folder. To be asked where to save each time (e.g. a specific folder), go to iPhone Settings → Safari → Downloads, and set it to "Ask".

"New Mob" clears the tally board and takes you back to Session Setup for the next batch.

Tips

  • Speak clearly, and pause briefly before and after each number.
  • Chrome tends to recognise voice a little more reliably than Safari, though both work.
  • If the mic stops listening on its own, just tap it again — that's a normal iPhone quirk, not a fault.
